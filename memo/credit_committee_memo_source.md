

# Credit committee memorandum



17 November 2026 | Independent project-finance case study | Information cutoff: 17 November 2026

Evaluate a hypothetical S$100m senior project-finance commitment to Project B Solar Portfolio SPV, an assumed Sembcorp-sponsored Singapore commercial rooftop solar portfolio. This is an educational simulation. DBS and Sembcorp are not participants, and no real financing request or bank policy is represented.

| Proposed transaction | Hypothetical terms |
| --- | --- |
| Asset / borrower | 120 MWp DC / 100 MWac across 60 assumed commercial rooftop sites; ring-fenced SPV. |
| Funding / repayment | S$153m planning envelope; S$53m permanent equity. Base actual uses S$143.15m and senior drawings S$90.15m. |
| Dates / pricing | Close 1 Jan 2027; base COD 1 Jul 2028; fixed maturity 30 Jun 2043. 60 quarterly principal payments; illustrative 4.5% base interest, with 70% benchmark hedging after COD. |
| Revenue / recourse | 80% as-generated production contracted for 20 years; 20% merchant. No full operating parent guarantee. |



## Decision rationale



1. The original covenant fails in the base case. Minimum organic quarterly DSCR is -2.89x when year-12 replacement is paid, against the assumed 1.20x requirement. The excluding-replacement minimum of 1.54x is a diagnostic and cannot replace the covenant test.

2. Downside cash payment failure is visible. The combined downside requires S$5.69m of hypothetical completion support and produces S$0.22m of temporary debt-service arrears with operating cures disabled. Eventual repayment at maturity does not erase that failure.

3. Completion funding is not robust. The severe case exhausts S$20m of assumed completion support and retains a S$1.13m gap. COD also crosses the fixed longstop. Its operating forecasts are conditional diagnostics, not evidence that construction can finish.

Model references: Dashboard E8:E25; Operations AZ39, AZ50, AZ52; Cash_Flow arrears and reserve schedules. All project-specific inputs and covenants are hypothetical. A balanced budget is not proof of bankability.

## Post-acquisition leverage matters



June 2026 company-style net debt was S$13,856m, compared with S$7,847m at December 2025, following Alinta funding and debt consolidation. Interim operating cash flow fell to S$319m from S$672m in 1H2025. The full acquired debt balance is included, while reported interim earnings contain only one acquisition month. Do not simply annualise that earnings base. [S03,S04]

Management separately reports annualised underlying pro-forma net debt / adjusted EBITDA of 5.3x and net debt / EBITDA of 6.1x, with stated environmental-certificate exclusions. These are issuer measures, not independent cash-coverage calculations. [S05, slide14]



## Liquidity and competing obligations



The standalone parent disclosed S$101m of cash at June 2026, versus S$164m at December 2025. Consolidated cash of S$1,334m is not equivalent to freely upstreamable parent cash. June borrowings due within one year were S$1,420m; unprovided commitments totalled S$1,290m. Parent guarantees overlap group financing and should not be added mechanically to consolidated debt. [S03, pp13-15,29]

For FY2025, OCF less cash PPE/investment-property and intangible capex was S$208m before cash interest. Adding dividends received and deducting cash interest and lease principal produces a S$55m cash-surplus proxy before acquisitions, equity investments and owner distributions. This is not the company-reported FCF measure. [S01; Sponsor schedule]

Conclusion: require documented project equity and capped support rather than rely on reputation or consolidated size. The S$53m equity, S$15m GST bridge, S$20m completion support and S$10m cure capacity in this case are assumptions, not actual Sembcorp promises.

## Coverage and liquidity are different



Base retained cash pays scheduled debt despite the replacement-quarter organic DSCR breach. The model deducts S$8.21m of replacement spending in CFADS, excludes reserve draws/cures from CFADS, and locks distributions using trailing and forward coverage tests. Base debt-service arrears are zero and senior debt is repaid by fixed maturity. The contractual breach still requires a revised, validated structure or an explicitly documented covenant treatment.

After PPA expiry, all production becomes merchant. The baseline also shows a S$1.50m end-of-life operating funding gap after loan maturity, which requires lifecycle funding but is not a senior debt-service shortfall.

## Decision-useful sensitivities



The full covenant already fails at baseline. Excluding only the planned replacement quarter, a generation multiplier of approximately 80.5% reduces minimum DSCR to 1.20x. Even a zero merchant reference leaves this diagnostic at 1.33x because 80% of production remains fixed-price contracted. These are conditional mathematical limits, not certified resource cases or market forecasts.

Native two-variable tables show merchant price versus generation and floating-rate shock versus operating cost. They use the excluding-replacement diagnostic to make ordinary operating headroom readable; the full negative quarter remains visible on the dashboard. A higher interest denominator can make a negative DSCR appear less negative, so that ratio alone is not evidence of lower risk.



## Sizing does not solve the lifecycle event



A diagnostic downside rerun raises permanent equity to S$64.62m and reduces actual debt to S$93.04m to reach 1.20x in ordinary quarters. The full minimum still fails and temporary arrears remain. This is not an approvable debt size. With the original quarterly CFADS definition and replacement during debt life, a lower loan alone cannot repair a negative replacement-quarter numerator.



# Required restructuring and controls



Decision: reject the present structure and invite a revised submission. This recommendation concerns the simulated structure and documented assumptions, not the creditworthiness of an actual Sembcorp transaction. No unconditional or conditional real-world approval is given.

| Requirement before reconsideration | Credit purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Fund the full asset lifecycle | Establish a legally controlled replacement/decommissioning reserve and validate its funding schedule. Agree a covenant basis that recognises funded lifecycle obligations without double-counting costs or inflating organic CFADS. Retain the full cash-flow view. |
| Re-size against the revised downside | Test every service period, payment liquidity, reserve adequacy and construction funding. Do not present the ordinary-quarter sizing diagnostic as final capacity. Cap draws at verified actual uses within the commitment. |
| Document sponsor obligations | Verify permanent equity and the separate GST/completion/cure facilities, their timing and caps. No unlimited support or freely upstreamable group cash assumption. |
| Resolve completion and offtake | Independent resource and engineering reports; site/roof rights, structural/grid review, relevant licences, EPC/O&M terms, signed PPAs, offtaker credit and concentration review. Any longstop waiver must be explicit. |



## Illustrative covenant and security package



Starting terms for a resubmission are minimum quarterly DSCR 1.20x and distribution tests at 1.30x, subject to an explicitly agreed lifecycle definition. Require a DSRA for the next two quarters of scheduled principal and net interest, 70% benchmark hedging and a 70% maximum commitment-to-defined-funded-cost ratio. These are hypothetical thresholds, not DBS policy. LTV is unavailable without a defensible valuation.

Require share, asset and controlled-account security; assignments of PPAs, rooftop rights and insurance; direct agreements and step-in rights; monthly construction and quarterly operating/covenant reporting. Block distributions for breaches, arrears, reserve deficits or unresolved completion gaps. A cash cure cannot rewrite historical organic DSCR; any remedy and 30-day process needs explicit legal documentation.
